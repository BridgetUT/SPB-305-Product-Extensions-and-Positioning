Sport Product Extensions

Interactive Critical Thinking Activities

The Sports Marketer's Role

The sports marketer's role is to create and use product extensions to help reach organizational goals and objectives while bringing fans positive and unique experiences that complement the core product.

Critical Thinking Activities

Core Concepts Recap

Key Insight: What Can Sports Marketers Control?

Sports marketers have limited control over core elements (the game itself, star power, rules) but significant control over product extensions that enhance the fan experience.

Product Extensions: Things That Enhance the Experience

Memories/Nostalgia

Definition: Memorabilia, collectibles, historic events

Examples: Commemorative jerseys, bobbleheads, hall of fame displays, retired number ceremonies, anniversary celebrations, vintage ticket stubs, player autograph sessions

Tickets, Programs, Other Printed Materials

Definition: Physical artifacts that serve as keepsakes

Examples: Collectible tickets, game programs with player stats, stadium maps, promotional flyers, scorecards, commemorative posters, media guides

Novelties, Fantasies

Definition: Unique experiences that fulfill fan dreams

Examples: Fantasy camps, meet-and-greets with players, on-field experiences, throw out first pitch opportunities, behind-the-scenes tours, luxury experiences

Digital/Electronic Products

Definition: Technology-enhanced fan experiences

Examples: Mobile apps, streaming services, virtual reality experiences, social media content, fantasy sports integration, digital collectibles, interactive games

Luxury Boxes

Definition: Premium seating and hospitality experiences

Examples: Private suites, club seating, VIP lounges, premium dining options, dedicated concierge service, exclusive parking, private entrances

Video Screens

Definition: Large displays enhancing the viewing experience

Examples: Jumbotrons, ribbon boards, interactive displays, instant replay screens, fan cam features, live social media feeds, sponsor content displays

Music/Entertainment

Definition: Audio and performance elements that enhance atmosphere

Examples: Walk-up music, halftime shows, live bands, DJ performances, crowd participation songs, victory celebrations, themed music nights

Mascots

Definition: Character representations that embody team spirit

Examples: Team mascots, mascot races, kids' birthday parties, community appearances, social media personalities, interactive games with fans

Official Fan Groups

Definition: Organized communities that build fan loyalty

Examples: Season ticket holder clubs, kids clubs, booster clubs, alumni groups, supporter sections, fan rewards programs, exclusive events

Key Learning Point:

Fan segmentation helps you understand how important these extensions are to different fans and how to use them as marketing tools. Different fan segments will value different extensions - families might prioritize mascots and kids clubs, while corporate clients might focus on luxury boxes and premium experiences.

Activity 1: New MLS Franchise Strategy

Scenario: You're the Marketing Director

Congratulations! You've just been hired as the marketing director for a brand new MLS franchise in a competitive sports market. Your team is young, unknown, and you need to build a fanbase from scratch.

Strategic Challenge:

What digital and electronic product extensions would be your top 3 priorities to develop fan engagement and differentiate your franchise?

Think Critically:

Consider:

  • What are your biggest challenges as a new franchise?
  • Who is your target audience in this market?
  • How can technology help you compete with established teams?
  • What would make fans choose YOU over other entertainment options?

Rank Your Top 3 Priorities

Review the three product extension options below, then assign each one a priority ranking. 1 = highest priority for launching this new franchise.

Hyper-Local Community App

An app that connects fans to local businesses, player community involvement, and neighborhood pride.

Interactive Stadium Technology

AR experiences, order-to-seat concessions, instant replays on personal devices in the stands.

Content Creation Platform

Behind-the-scenes access, player vlogs, and fan-generated content contests.

Explain why you ordered these priorities the way you did. What makes your #1 choice the most urgent for a brand-new franchise?

One Expert's Strategic Framework:

Hyper-Local Community App
Create an app that connects fans to local businesses, player community involvement, and neighborhood pride. New franchises need deep community roots.

Interactive Stadium Technology
AR experiences, order-to-seat concessions, instant replays on personal devices. Make the in-stadium experience uniquely high-tech.

Content Creation Platform
Behind-the-scenes access, player vlogs, fan-generated content contests. Build emotional connections when you can't rely on winning tradition.

Why This Framework Works: It addresses the new franchise's core challenges - unknown players (content access), no tradition (community focus), and competing entertainment options (technology differentiation).

There's no single correct order here — compare your reasoning to this framework and think about where and why your priorities differ.

Activity 2: LPGA vs NFL Differentiation Strategy

Comparative Analysis Challenge

The positioning map below shows how LPGA and NFL differ on pace of action and fan intimacy. Study it, then analyze the strategic implications:

LPGA Advantages:

  • High skill, slower pace (easier to follow action)
  • Fans can move with players around course
  • Very close proximity to action
  • High chance of player interaction

NFL Advantages:

  • High skill, high pace (exciting action)
  • Uniform stadium experience
  • Established traditions and pageantry
  • Mass media appeal

Critical Analysis Questions:

1. If you were marketing the LPGA, which differentiators would you emphasize most and why?

2. What differentiators should the LPGA avoid highlighting?

3. How would your strategy change for different fan segments?

Strategic Thinking Exercise:

Consider how these differentiators appeal to different consumer values:

  • Intimacy seekers: Want personal connections
  • Action junkies: Want excitement and energy
  • Social fans: Want shared experiences
  • Purists: Want skill and strategy

Strategic Differentiation Analysis:

LPGA Should Emphasize:

  • Player accessibility - "Meet your heroes" positioning
  • Skill appreciation - "See every shot up close"
  • Premium experience - "Intimate, exclusive sporting event"

LPGA Should Avoid:

  • Comparing pace directly to other sports (plays into "boring" stereotype)
  • Emphasizing smaller crowds (could seem less important)

Key Insight: Turn potential weaknesses into strengths. "Slower pace" becomes "appreciate the skill," "smaller crowds" becomes "intimate experience."

Activity 3: Perceptual Mapping Exercise

FC Cincinnati Competitive Analysis

You work for FC Cincinnati. Consider how consumers perceive your team compared to other entertainment options using "Excitement" vs "Hassle Factor" dimensions.

Mapping Challenge:

Where would you place these Cincinnati entertainment options on an Excitement vs Hassle Factor map?

  • FC Cincinnati match
  • Cincinnati Reds game
  • Cincinnati Bengals game
  • Netflix at home
  • Cincinnati Symphony
  • Local bar with friends

Strategic Questions:

  • If FC Cincinnati is in the "High Hassle" quadrants, what product extensions could reduce perceived hassle?
  • If in "Low Excitement" quadrants, what extensions could increase excitement?
  • What would be the ideal position for FC Cincinnati?
  • How might different fan segments perceive these attributes differently?

Perceptual Mapping Insights:

High Excitement, Low Hassle (Sweet Spot): Netflix at home, well-organized events with easy access

High Excitement, High Hassle: Bengals games (traffic, crowds, cost), major concerts

Low Excitement, Low Hassle: Symphony, casual dining, coffee shops

Low Excitement, High Hassle: Poorly organized events (avoid this!)

Strategic Goal: Move FC Cincinnati toward the sweet spot through extensions that reduce hassle (mobile tickets, express concessions, parking solutions) while maintaining/increasing excitement (fan interactions, entertainment, technology).

Activity 4: Innovation Evaluation Framework

Real-World Innovation Analysis

The NBA's "99-cent 4th quarter" streaming option represents a product development innovation. Let's critically evaluate it using innovation theory.

Innovation Evaluation Criteria

Analyze the NBA's 99-cent 4th quarter option using the five key innovation dimensions:

Your Innovation Challenge:

Rate this innovation on:

  • Relative Advantage: How much better than existing options?
  • Complexity: How difficult to adopt?
  • Compatibility: How well does it fit consumer values?
  • Divisibility: Can consumers try it in small portions?
  • Communicability: How easy to explain benefits?

NBA Innovation Evaluation:

High Relative Advantage: Cheaper than full game, captures most exciting part, perfect for casual fans

Low Complexity: Simple one-click purchase, familiar interface, no learning curve

High Compatibility: Matches modern consumption (micro-transactions, on-demand, mobile-first)

High Divisibility: Low cost trial, specific time segment, minimal risk

High Communicability: Simple concept - "Watch the most exciting part for under a dollar"

Prediction: This innovation should succeed because it scores highly on all five adoption criteria, addressing modern viewing habits and removing barriers to entry.

Activity 5: Strategic Decision Making - The Extension Dilemma

Budget Allocation Challenge

You're the marketing director for a mid-market MLB team. You have $500,000 to spend on ONE major product extension. Your fan research shows three distinct segments with different preferences.

Fan Segments:

  • Family Fans (40%): Want safe, fun experiences for kids
  • Corporate Groups (25%): Want networking and hospitality
  • Die-Hard Fans (35%): Want authentic, team-focused experiences

Your Options:

  • Interactive Kids Zone: Appeals to families, some corporate groups
  • Premium Rooftop Experience: Appeals to corporate groups, affluent die-hards
  • Team History Museum: Appeals to die-hards, moderate appeal to others

Strategic Decision Framework:

Kids Zone Analysis: Targets largest segment (40%), creates future fans, generates additional revenue streams, differentiates from competitors

Rooftop Analysis: Highest revenue per person, attracts business events, creates premium positioning, year-round usage

Museum Analysis: Broad appeal across segments, strengthens team connection, educational value, tourism potential

Critical Questions to Consider:

  • Which segment drives the most revenue per person?
  • Which segment has the most growth potential?
  • What are the long-term strategic implications of each choice?
  • Could you modify any option to appeal to multiple segments?
  • What does this decision say about your brand positioning?

Synthesis Challenge: Your Strategic Recommendation

Final Critical Thinking Exercise

You've analyzed product extensions, differentiation, positioning, and innovation. Now synthesize your learning into a strategic recommendation.

The Situation:

A minor league hockey team in a college town is struggling with attendance. They compete with:

  • College basketball (high excitement, school loyalty)
  • Movie theaters (low hassle, affordable)
  • Restaurants/bars (social, convenient)
  • Streaming at home (very low hassle, personalized)

Your Strategic Challenge:

Develop a comprehensive product extension strategy that addresses:

  1. Differentiation: What makes hockey unique in this market?
  2. Positioning: Where should you position on excitement vs. hassle?
  3. Product Development: What 3 extensions would you prioritize?
  4. Innovation: How would you test and implement these extensions?

Framework for Your Analysis:

  • Consider the college demographic and their preferences
  • Think about hockey's unique attributes (speed, physicality, skill)
  • Address the "hassle factors" that might deter attendance
  • Leverage the social aspects that appeal to students
  • Consider budget constraints of your target market

Present your strategy as if you're pitching to the team's ownership group. Be specific, be strategic, and justify your decisions.
